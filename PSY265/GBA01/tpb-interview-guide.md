# HSG Screening Uptake — TPB Interview Guide
### PSY265 GBA01, Group of 5

## A note on AI use boundaries for this assignment

The GBA01 brief limits GenAI to: brainstorming ideas/outlines, summarising research for reference, and fixing minor spelling/grammar — all with disclosure. It rules out wholesale AI-generated content, AI-generated references, and AI doing the rewriting/restructuring of your write-up.

What that means in practice for this guide: it's fine to use an AI-drafted question set as your starting point for brainstorming (that's exactly what this is), but your group should review, adapt, and finalise the wording yourselves, conduct and write up the interviews in your own words, and disclose the AI assistance per the format your instructor linked in Announcements. The actual interview responses, the demographic table, the TPB analysis linking your findings to the campaign, and the reference list all need to come from your own work — not generated.

## Why semi-structured

With only 5 interviews, a semi-structured format is the right call: fixed core questions per TPB construct so the 5 interviews are comparable, but open enough to probe unexpected barriers or motivators as they come up. Plan roughly 15–20 minutes per interview.

## Eligibility screen (ask before starting)

- Are you 40 years of age or above?
- Have you *not* gone for a basic health screening or cancer screening (e.g. Pap smear, mammogram, colorectal, PSA) in the past three years?

Only proceed if both are confirmed. No names are recorded — assign each interviewee an ID (P1–P5) for your table.

## Opening / rapport

1. Just to start, when you hear "health screening" — basic health checks or cancer screening — what comes to mind for you?
2. Have you ever gone for any kind of health screening before, even a long time ago? What was that like?

## Attitude (beliefs & evaluation of the behaviour)

3. What do you see as the benefits, if any, of going for a health screening at this point in your life?
4. What concerns or downsides do you associate with screening — for example discomfort, waiting for results, cost, or something else?
5. Overall, would you say you feel positive, negative, or neutral about health screening? What makes you feel that way?
6. Is there anything about screening specifically (versus other health check-ups) that changes how you feel about it?

## Subjective norms (perceived social pressure/support)

7. Are there people in your life — family, friends, colleagues, or your doctor — who think you should go for screening?
8. Has anyone close to you ever brought up screening with you directly, either encouraging or discouraging you?
9. Do you know people around your age who've gone for screening? Does knowing that affect how you think about it, one way or another?
10. If someone important to you strongly wanted you to go for screening, would that change your decision? Why or why not?

## Perceived behavioural control (perceived ease/difficulty, barriers, self-efficacy)

11. If you decided today that you wanted to go for a screening, how easy or difficult would that be for you to actually arrange?
12. What tends to get in the way — time, cost, distance/location, fear of results, not knowing where to go, or something else?
13. Have you ever tried or planned to book a screening before? What happened?
14. How confident are you that you could get a screening done in the next 3 months if you decided to? What would make you more confident?

## Behavioural intention (optional, but useful for tying constructs to intention)

15. Do you think you'll go for a screening in the next 6–12 months? What would need to happen for that to be a "yes"?

## Closing — barriers, concerns, motivators (directly required by the brief)

16. Thinking back over everything we've discussed, what's the single biggest reason you haven't gone for screening in the past three years?
17. What would motivate or encourage you to finally go — a person, a reminder, cost subsidy, convenience, something else?
18. Is there anything about Healthier SG (HSG) screening specifically — cost, location, subsidy, having a regular clinic — that would make a difference for you?
19. Is there anything else about screening you'd like to share that we haven't covered?

Thank the participant and remind them no identifying information is being recorded.

## Post-baseline probe (ask ONLY after everything above, never before)

Everything up to this point captures the interviewee's genuine, unprompted attitude, subjective norms, and PBC — that's your baseline evidence and it needs to stay uncontaminated. Do **not** mention cost, convenience, or ease of screening before this point, or you'll be measuring their reaction to your pitch instead of their actual barrier.

Once the baseline questions are done, you can add this as a distinct, clearly separate final step:

20. "Just to check something — if you knew that a basic screening costs about $X and takes around Y minutes at [a nearby HSG clinic], would that change how you feel about it, or would your main concern still be there?"

This isn't a barrier question — it's a diagnostic for your campaign design. It tells you whether a given interviewee's barrier is *informational* (an "I didn't realise it was that easy/cheap" reaction — fixable by a message like your promotional video) versus something a cost/convenience fact doesn't move (fear, taboo, time scarcity, distrust) — which instead needs a different kind of persuasive or structural approach. Log the response separately from the baseline PBC answer (question 11–14) in the findings table below, so the two never get conflated when your Slide Lead writes up the TPB mapping.
